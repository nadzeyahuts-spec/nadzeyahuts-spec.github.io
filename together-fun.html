Background

Together.fun is a Play-to-Earn game on Solana. Players stake SOL, choose a sector, and win proportionally. The product existed as a smart contract and 45 pages of Confluence specs — but zero UX. No wireframes, no user flows, no information hierarchy.

This was my first game project. I had no portfolio work in gaming, no industry connections, no internal mentor. I don't even play games — unless you count my Duolingo streak (learning English). But I had 6 years of product design experience in complex systems — RWA tokenization, DAO governance, neobanks — and 16 years of graphic design. I drew the pixel-accurate game graphics myself.

Challenge

Turn complex DeFi mechanics into an experience that feels like a game, not a spreadsheet. Make the session key (double-signature) feel like a feature, not a technical hurdle.

What I Started With

I have this game idea. The mechanic is simple – pick a sector, wait for the timer, win or lose. People can win real crypto. I think players will love it. But the problem is trust. They need to believe it's not a scam, that it's pure luck. That's what I need from you – make them trust it.

— Stakeholder interview, October 2025

The CANCELLED state alone — when fewer than 2 sectors fill — was undocumented from a UX perspective entirely. That's a moment where someone's SOL is frozen mid-play.

That screen needs to earn trust, not create panic.

Hidden UX Gaps

When the BA and I started mapping the client's Confluence specs into actual user journeys, we realised something was missing.

The client had written about emotions ("90% get rekt, 10% get rich"). The devs had written about states (GATHERING → COUNTDOWN → LOCKED…).

But three critical moments had no UX at all.

01

The Session Key Moment

The client wrote: "YOLO your SOL on the project you simps for." What wasn't written: the player's first deposit requires two Phantom signatures. For a casual gamer, this looks like a phishing attempt.

We caught this during journey mapping.

02

The Waiting Zone

The client described the game flow: "Pick your poison from 9 sections and ape in your SOL." But nothing described what happens between deposit and sector selection. Players land in a "waiting zone" with a ticking timer and no context.

The BA flagged it as an undefined state. I designed the missing experience.

03

The Cancelled Round

The client's tagline: "90% get rekt, 10% get rich." But what happens when fewer than 2 sectors fill? The smart contract cancels the round. The spec had no screen for this.

We found this gap together — then I built the solution

3×3 Sector Grid — Meme Coin Themes

Two game modes:

  • 1/9 — one winner takes the pool from 8 losing sectors.
  • 8/9 — one unlucky sector funds everyone else.

The social dynamic: watch odds shift in real time as players pile into sectors, then decide when to join or switch — auto-signed by session key, no popup.

That sector-switch mechanic — seamless, instant, no wallet interruption — is the product's killer feature. Communicating its existence and safety was a central UX challenge.

What the creator configures:

room name, game mode, min stake, max participants, start time, room/round lifetime, access settings, creator fee (0–10%).

Phase 5

User Flow

After mapping three personas, I chose Gamer Mia's first deposit and play as the central flow.

  • Business impactFirst deposit is the conversion moment. If users drop off here, they rarely return.
  • Highest UX riskContains the product's most critical pain points: wallet fear, double-signature confusion, undefined waiting state.
  • MeasurabilityThis flow allows clear A/B testing with a concrete metric: deposit completion rate.

I designed for the most anxious user. Confident ones adapt. Anxious ones leave.

Based on the IA and research insights, I mapped this critical user path. The flow shows how Mia navigates the system to achieve her goal.

Goal: Complete first deposit and play

  • Mia needs the session key explained as a benefit, not a technical requirement
  • The "waiting zone" must show clear status and next steps
  • Every edge case (cancelled round, transaction failure) needs a designed screen to maintain trust

Approach

My approach followed atomic design methodology: starting with foundational elements and progressively combining them into complex components.

The Foundation

Atoms — The Foundation

I began by defining the smallest, indivisible UI elements using Figma Variables and Fonts style for consistent theming.

Built atomic components — pixel buttons (4 states, with/without icons), dropdowns, menu items, and hand-drawn pixel icons.

The Foundation

Molecules — Combining Primitives

At the molecule level, I combined atoms into functional units.

Complex Components

Organisms

One of the most complex UI challenges was the NFT avatar system. Instead of designing each NFT individually (which would have been time-prohibitive), I created a 6-layer nested component system:

How it works:

  • Each layer is an independent nested variant in Figma
  • Any combination of layers can be previewed instantly
  • The system caught visual clashes early, before any pixel art was finalized

Why this approach:

  • Scalability: 6 layers × 5–8 options per layer = 15,625 to 262,144 possible combinations — without designing a single complete avatar manually.
  • Dev handoff: Engineers received a clear specification of how layers stack
  • QA efficiency: Every permutation is automatically consistent — no manual checking needed

Product Features

The Core Game Screen

Room Details

Main Screen — The Game Room

The main gameplay screen where players view live odds, select a sector, stake SOL, and track the round's 8-phase lifecycle, all while seeing their personal position in real-time.

UX Value: Solves two key problems: replaces anxiety-inducing percentages with a visual heat map and provides a clear 8-state progress strip so players always understand "what's happening now."

Place a bet

Players pick a sector, choose a preset or custom amount, and confirm their bid. Quick-bet multipliers (1×/5×/10×) and a clear minimum keep staking fast and predictable.

UX Value: The platform fee is shown as "applied on win only", and the deposit is framed as a single calm confirmation — removing the fear that a wrong tap will cost real money.

Win

If the player wins, a popup celebrates the win, shows the payout amount, and includes a link to verify the result on Solscan. Transparency builds trust — especially for crypto-savvy users.

Loss

If the player loses, the popup shows what happened and the fee breakdown. Instead of making players feel frustrated, it gives them one clear button: "Play Again." The loss screen is actually the retention screen.

This flow solved three key problems — players were confused by wallet approvals, anxious about percentages, and unsure what was happening between placing a bet and seeing the result. By making every step clear and transparent, players actually trust the game enough to keep playing.

The hub where players find games

Rooms — From Browsing to Creating

Browse Rooms

Players see all available rooms in one place with tabs for Active, Upcoming, My Rooms, and Invites.

Each card shows key info: game mode, player count, SOL pool, and time left. Hover or tap a card and it flips to show extra details like creator fee and min stake — clean and uncluttered.

My Rooms (Creator Dashboard)

Creators see all their rooms in one tab. They can track how many players joined, see earnings from creator fees, and claim rewards with one click. No more digging through menus.

Create a Room

Eligible players (Level 2+) fill out a simple form to set up a custom room: choose game mode, set stake and player limits, pick a start time, and set a creator fee (0–10%). The form shows an estimated yield in real time — so creators know what they'll earn before launching.

Closed Rounds

A complete history of every finished round. Players can search by round ID, room name, or creator, and filter by game type, date range, or show only rounds they joined. Each row shows total bank, player count, winner sector, and number of winners — click any row to verify results on Solscan.

These screens fixed three problems — creators couldn't find their earnings, nobody knew what they'd earn before creating a room, and players had no way to verify past results. Now everything is visible, accessible, and transparent. When players can see the history, they trust the game more — and trust is everything in crypto.

From quests to rewards

Achievements & NFTs

Browse Achievements

Players see all available quests grouped by type:

  • Social (follow on X, join Discord),
  • Gameplay (win games, play streaks),
  • Financial (deposit SOL, hit profit targets).

Each card shows rarity (from Common to Legendary), progress, XP reward, and unlock rate — so you know how rare and how close you are.

Progression System:

a reward loop that turns player actions into XP and NFT rewards, keeping players engaged beyond the core game.

Rarity types:

Claim XP Reward

When an achievement is completed, players instantly earn XP.

XP is what levels you up — higher levels unlock more features (like creating rooms) and better rewards.

A popup confirms the reward with a clear "Claim" button.

Unlock, Mint & Reveal NFT

Completing certain achievements (like reaching Level 3, 6, or 9) rewards an NFT avatar.

To reveal it, players mint it on-chain — this costs only a small network fee (no platform fee). The rarity (Common → Legendary) is randomly assigned at mint time using verifiable randomness, so everyone has a fair shot at something epic.

After minting, the avatar is fully revealed with its rarity, XP bonus (e.g., +15% XP for Legendary), and transaction hash for verification. Players can equip it immediately or view it in their collection.

Achievements give players a reason to keep coming back — not just for the game itself, but for the rewards and status. The NFT system adds real value: rarity tiers, verifiable ownership, and XP bonuses that make your avatar actually useful. Every step from quest to equipped NFT is designed to feel like progress, not paperwork.

Profile & social

Identity, Notifications & Sharing

User Profile

Players see everything about their account in one place:

  • avatar, nickname, wallet address, and core stats (level, games played/won, total deposits, rewards, net P/L).
  • The XP progress bar shows how close they are to the next level.
  • Claimables (payouts, refunds, referral rewards) are surfaced with a single "Claim All" button — no more hunting through menus.

Avatar Management

Players can change their avatar from a modal that shows both default presets and NFT avatars they've earned.

NFTs are filterable by rarity (Common → Legendary) and include XP bonus info. If you hold the NFT at game lock, the bonus applies — so your avatar actually matters for gameplay.

Notifications Hub

A single feed for everything that needs the player's attention: payouts received, rounds finished, daily tasks completed, reward and level claims, room starts, achievements and rank changes.

Each item carries a direct action — Claim, Open Room, View Leaderboard — and "Mark all as read" / "Clear" keep the hub tidy. No event gets lost.

Share Your Stats

Players can share their progress on X (Twitter) with one click. A modal shows a pre-filled message with their stats, XP earned, and a share button. This helps players show off and brings new users into the game.

These screens turn the game from a solo experience into a social one. Players can see their progress, show off their avatars, stay on top of rewards, and invite friends — all from one place. It's where identity meets engagement.
